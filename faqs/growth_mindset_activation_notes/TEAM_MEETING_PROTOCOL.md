# TEAM MEETING OPENING PROTOCOL
## Weekly Growth Frame Reinforcement

**Context:** 20-25 minute team meetings outside class time  
**Frequency:** Weekly (or bi-weekly per team)  
**Purpose:** Maintain growth frame, address blockers in outcome frame, build momentum

---

## The Non-Negotiable Opening

### THE QUESTION

Every meeting starts with this question. No exceptions.

> **"What's one thing that's working better than last week?"**

This is not a warmup. This is not optional. This is the intervention.

### WHY IT WORKS

| Mechanism | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| **Presupposes improvement** | They must search for evidence of progress to answer |
| **Primes resourceful state** | Accessing "what's working" before discussing problems |
| **Sets outcome frame** | Meeting begins focused on progress, not complaints |
| **Pattern interrupt** | Teams naturally default to problems; this breaks that |
| **Accumulates evidence** | Weekly, they build a stack of "things that improved" |

---

## Handling Resistance

Students will try to dodge this question. Here are your responses:

### "Nothing is better."

**As-If Frame:**
> "If something were 2% better—even something tiny—what might it be?"

**Chunk Down:**
> "What about just your dev environment? Is that more stable than Week 1?"

**Comparison Frame:**
> "Compare right now to Day 1. Where are you different?"

**Challenge the Absolute:**
> "Literally nothing changed? No new code, no new knowledge, nothing? Same exact state as last week?"

### "Everything is terrible."

**Acknowledge, then redirect:**
> "Sounds like a hard week. And—what's one thing that's even slightly less terrible than it was?"

**Specify the outcome:**
> "What would 'less terrible' look like for next week?"

### Silence / Blank looks

**Wait longer than is comfortable.** Then:
> "Take a minute. Think about what you worked on. What moved forward, even a little?"

## Meeting Structure Template

| Time | Phase | Focus |
|------|-------|-------|
| 0:00 - 3:00 | **Opening** | "What's working better?" |
| 3:00 - 5:00 | **Wins Recognition** | Acknowledge responses, link to Growth Signature |
| 5:00 - 15:00 | **Blockers → Solutions** | "What's in the way and how do we move it?" |
| 15:00 - 22:00 | **Work Discussion** | Specific technical or team issues |
| 22:00 - 25:00 | **Closing** | "What's the ONE thing for next week?" |

---

## Phase Details

### OPENING (0:00 - 3:00)

Ask the question. Go around the team. Everyone answers.

If someone says "I'll pass" or "nothing":
- Come back to them at the end
- Use the resistance responses above
- Don't let anyone fully skip—even "my laptop didn't crash this week" counts

### WINS RECOGNITION (3:00 - 5:00)

Brief acknowledgment of what they shared. Optional Growth Signature callback:

> "So we've got [X], [Y], and [Z] improvements this week. The Algorithm is detecting positive trajectory."

Don't overdo this. Simple acknowledgment, then move on.

### BLOCKERS → SOLUTIONS (5:00 - 15:00)

**Frame matters here.** The question is NOT "What's going wrong?"

The question IS:
> "What's blocking progress, and what could unblock it?"

Or:
> "What's in the way, and how do we move it?"

**For each blocker:**
1. Identify it specifically (not "everything")
2. Ask: "What would help with this?"
3. Determine: Is this something they can solve, or do they need resources?
4. Assign action: Who does what by when?

**Language shift:**
- NOT: "Why is this broken?"
- YES: "How could we fix this?"
- NOT: "Who dropped the ball?"
- YES: "What needs to happen for this to move forward?"

### WORK DISCUSSION (15:00 - 22:00)

This is standard team meeting content:
- Technical questions
- Coordination issues
- Demo prep
- Sprint planning

Keep outcome frame throughout:
- "What do we want the result to be?"
- "How will we know it's done?"
- "What's the first step?"

### CLOSING (22:00 - 25:00)

The closing question:

> **"What's the ONE thing that, if you did it this week, would make the biggest difference?"**

Each person answers. This creates:
- Commitment (public statement)
- Focus (ONE thing, not twelve)
- Future-pacing (they've already imagined doing it)

End with:
> "Good. The Algorithm will be watching for commit activity. See you [next meeting]."

## Situational Responses

### Team is stuck in conflict

> "I hear there's tension. Let's set that aside for 10 minutes and focus on one question: What does this team need to function next week?"

Address relationship issues separately if needed. Don't let the team meeting become conflict mediation.

### One person is clearly struggling

Note it, don't call it out publicly. Follow up 1:1 after the meeting:

> "Hey, I noticed you seemed stuck back there. What's going on?"

Then use the 1:1 protocols from the office hours section.

### Team is crushing it, no problems

Celebrate briefly, then raise the bar:

> "Solid week. What would make next week even better? What could you stretch toward?"

Don't invent problems, but don't let coasting become the norm.

### Someone hasn't done anything since last meeting

**Don't:** "Why didn't you do anything?"

**Do:** "What got in the way this week? What would help you move forward?"

Listen for the actual blocker. It's rarely laziness. It's usually:
- Stuck and didn't ask for help
- Overwhelmed by something outside class
- Lost and didn't know where to start

Address at the right level.

## Tracking Progress

Consider keeping a simple log per team:

| Week | What's Better | Main Blocker | ONE Thing |
|------|---------------|--------------|-----------|
| 2 | Dev env setup | Merge conflicts | Establish branching strategy |
| 3 | Branching cleaner | API design | Complete endpoint spec |
| ... | ... | ... | ... |

This becomes useful for:
- Clearance promotion evidence
- Mid-semester check-ins
- End-of-course reflection ("Look how far you've come")

---

## The Meta Point

These meetings are not primarily about solving problems.

They are about **repeatedly accessing the growth state** and **accumulating evidence of improvement**.

Every week, they answer "what's better." Every week, they find something.

By Week 8, they have 8 weeks of evidence that they improve.

That evidence is more persuasive than any presentation you could give.

The meeting structure is the intervention. Trust it.

Adaptive math practice that meets you where you are.

Spring 2026 · FTCC

The Problem
Math practice has a motivation problem.

It's not that people can't do math. It's that practicing math is almost always boring, stressful, or both.

The current options: Worksheets → Boring. Nobody does them voluntarily. Timed tests → Stressful. Associates math with anxiety. Drill apps → Boring with better graphics. Gamified apps → Fun, but the math is an afterthought. The result: people avoid practice. The skills they once had get rusty.

This isn't just a kid problem. Adults lose mental math fluency. Seniors worry about cognitive decline. Everyone benefits from practice — if the practice doesn't feel like punishment.

The Problem, Continued
The difficulty problem.

Even when people try to practice, most tools get the difficulty wrong.

TOO EASY: "What's 2 + 3?" → Bored in 30 seconds → No learning happening → User quits ("this is for little kids")
TOO HARD: "Solve: 847 ÷ 23" → Frustrated immediately → Associates math with failure → User quits ("I'm bad at math")
The sweet spot — where you have to think but you can do it — is different for every person. And it shifts as they improve. Most tools don't adjust. DataMan does.

Who We're Building For
Two people. Same problem.
Mia, Age 10
5th grader · Reluctant Practicer
"I don't hate math. I hate worksheets."
Mia is average at math — not terrible, not great. Her mom wants her to practice at home, but worksheets turn into a nightly battle. She's tried three math apps. She used each one for about a week before getting bored because the problems were either too easy or too hard. She'd rather be drawing.
James, Age 34
Project Manager · Coffee Break Sharpener
"I play Wordle every morning. I'd do math puzzles if they took two minutes."
James hasn't done mental math since college. He uses a calculator for everything. He does the NYT crossword on his commute and plays Wordle before his first meeting — he likes short mental challenges that make him feel sharp. But every math app he's tried felt like it was designed for children.
The Solution
DataMan

A web-based math challenge platform with four game modes, adaptive difficulty, and a progress dashboard. Sessions take 2-5 minutes. The app gets harder when you're doing well and easier when you're struggling — automatically, invisibly.

Feature 1
Four Game Modes
Answer Checker, Number Guesser, Speed Round, Fill It In. Each one exercises a different kind of math thinking. Short sessions, immediate feedback.
Feature 2
Adaptive Difficulty
The app tracks your recent accuracy and adjusts automatically. Mia gets grade-appropriate problems. James gets challenged at his level. Same app.
Feature 3
Progress Dashboard
Streaks. Trends. Personal bests. The dashboard tells a story: "You're improving." Mia sees her streak. James sees his accuracy trend.
Feature 4
Persistent Accounts
Register, log in, pick up where you left off. Your history, your streak, your difficulty tier — all saved. Come back tomorrow and everything's still there.
How It Solves the Problem
Same app. Different experience.
Mia's experience:
Opens DataMan after dinner. Dashboard: "🔥 6-day streak!" Picks Speed Round. 60 seconds of multiplication. Gets 16 right — 2 more than yesterday. "New personal best!" Plays one more round. Total time: 4 minutes. Goes back to drawing. She practiced voluntarily.
James's experience:
Opens DataMan with coffee. Dashboard: "Accuracy ↑12% this month" Picks Answer Checker. 15 quick right/wrong decisions. Tier 5 problems — actually challenging. Done in 2 minutes. Closes tab before first meeting. He'll do it again tomorrow. He practiced voluntarily.

Both solved the same problem: math practice that doesn't feel like punishment. The adaptive difficulty made it personal. The dashboard made it visible.

Why Flask? The team knows Python from CTS 285. Flask is lightweight and well-documented. Why SQLite first? Zero config — it's a file on disk. We migrate to PostgreSQL when we deploy to Render, and SQLAlchemy makes that migration a config change, not a rewrite.

Each table answers one question. The users table handles auth. The sessions and responses tables record game history. The user_tiers table drives adaptive difficulty. The daily_stats table powers the dashboard trend chart.

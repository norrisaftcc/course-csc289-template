## 📋 OVERVIEW

This rubric evaluates your Week 4 Pitch Presentation. You are presenting to stakeholders (instructor, classmates, possibly design partners) to justify your project and demonstrate initial progress.

**The pitch presentation follows this required structure:**
1. **Problem Statement** (1-2 min) - What problem exists in the world?
2. **Team Introduction** (30 sec) - Who are you and what are your roles?
3. **User Personas** (1-2 min) - Who experiences this problem? Show 2 specific personas.
4. **Solution Overview** (2-3 min) - What are you building? How does it solve the problem?
5. **Live Demo** (2-3 min) - Show what you've built so far (even if minimal)
6. **Technical Architecture** (1-2 min) - How does it work under the hood?
7. **Next Steps** (30 sec) - What's coming in Sprint 2?
8. **Q&A** (2 min) - Answer stakeholder questions

**This rubric assigns points to everything that matters.** If it's not here, it's not graded. If it's here, do it.

## 🎤 SECTION 1: PRESENTATION FUNDAMENTALS (20 points)

These are professional presentation skills. They matter in every technical presentation you'll give in your career.

### Audio & Clarity (8 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (8) | Proficient (6) | Developing (4) | Insufficient (0-2) |
|----------|--------------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Volume & Articulation** | All speakers clearly audible throughout room. Words are distinct and well-paced. | Most speakers audible most of the time. Occasional mumbling or rushing. | Some speakers difficult to hear. Frequent mumbling or unclear speech. | Multiple speakers inaudible. Audience must strain to hear. |

**What we're looking for:**
- Can the audience in the back row hear you?
- Are you speaking clearly, not mumbling?
- Are you pacing your speech (not racing through slides)?

### Visual Contact & Engagement (6 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (6) | Proficient (4) | Developing (2) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Eye Contact & Body Language** | Speakers make regular eye contact with audience. Natural body language. Minimal reading from slides. | Some eye contact. Occasional slide reading. Generally confident posture. | Frequent slide reading. Limited eye contact. Nervous body language. | Constant reading from slides. No audience engagement. |

**What we're looking for:**
- Are you looking at the audience, not just your laptop?
- Are you using slides as a guide, not reading them verbatim?
- Are you standing/sitting with confident posture?

### Timing (6 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (6) | Proficient (4) | Developing (2) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Time Management** | 8-10 minutes total. All sections covered. Natural pacing. | 7-11 minutes. All sections present but some rushed or stretched. | 6-12 minutes. Some sections skipped or severely rushed. | Under 6 or over 12 minutes. Major sections missing or excessive filler. |

**What we're looking for:**
- Did you practice your timing?
- Do you transition smoothly between sections?
- Do you respect the 10-minute limit (leaving time for Q&A)?

## 📊 SECTION 2: CONTENT STRUCTURE (25 points)

Your pitch must follow the required structure. Points are deducted for missing or out-of-order sections.

### Problem Statement (5 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (5) | Proficient (3-4) | Developing (1-2) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Problem Clarity** | Clear, specific problem stated upfront. Audience understands why this matters. Real-world context provided. | Problem stated but lacks specificity or context. Somewhat clear why it matters. | Vague problem statement. Unclear why audience should care. | No clear problem statement OR problem stated after solution. |

**Required elements:**
- State the problem BEFORE introducing yourselves or the solution
- Explain who experiences this problem
- Explain why current solutions are inadequate
- Make it specific (not "task management is hard" but "freelancers struggle to track client deliverables across multiple projects")

### Team Introduction (3 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (3) | Proficient (2) | Developing (1) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Team Presentation** | All members introduced with names and roles. Confident delivery. | Most members introduced. Roles somewhat clear. | Incomplete introductions. Roles unclear. | No team introduction OR completely unclear who is who. |

**Required elements:**
- Every team member's name
- Each person's primary role (frontend, backend, database, etc.)
- Brief (30 seconds total - don't belabor this)

### User Personas (7 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (7) | Proficient (5-6) | Developing (3-4) | Insufficient (0-2) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Persona Development** | 2 distinct, specific personas presented. Clear names, contexts, and pain points. Audience can visualize these users. | 2 personas present but lack detail or distinction. Somewhat generic. | Only 1 persona OR both very generic (e.g., "college student who needs to study"). | No personas OR completely abstract/vague. |

**Required elements:**
- **TWO personas** (not one, not three)
- Each has a name (not "User A")
- Each has specific context (age, situation, specific problem they face)
- Each demonstrates HOW they experience the problem
- Presented BEFORE the solution

**Example of a good persona:**
> "Meet Sarah, a freelance graphic designer managing 8 client projects simultaneously. She currently uses a spreadsheet to track deliverables, but clients keep emailing her 'Where's my logo?' because her spreadsheet isn't connected to her calendar or email. She misses deadlines because she forgets to check the spreadsheet."

**Example of a weak persona:**
> "User 1 is a freelancer who needs to manage tasks."

### Solution Overview (5 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (5) | Proficient (3-4) | Developing (1-2) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Solution Explanation** | Clear explanation of WHAT you're building. Key features identified. Explains HOW it solves the stated problem. | Solution described but connection to problem unclear. Features listed but not explained. | Vague solution description. Unclear what it actually does. | No solution overview OR solution doesn't match stated problem. |

**Required elements:**
- Explain what your platform IS (task manager, e-commerce store, etc.)
- Identify 3-5 key features
- Explicitly connect features back to persona pain points
- Explain the white-label concept (multiple themed versions)

### Next Steps/Roadmap (5 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (5) | Proficient (3-4) | Developing (1-2) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Sprint 2 Planning** | Clear roadmap for next 2-3 sprints. Realistic scope. Prioritized features. | Some roadmap provided. May lack specificity or realistic scope. | Vague "we'll add more features" without detail. | No roadmap OR completely unrealistic (e.g., "we'll add AI and blockchain next week"). |

**Required elements:**
- What features are coming in Sprint 2 specifically?
- What are stretch goals for Sprints 3-4?
- Any known technical challenges you're planning to tackle?

## 💻 SECTION 3: TECHNICAL DEMONSTRATION (25 points)

You must show working code. Even if minimal, something must run.

### Demo Functionality (15 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (15) | Proficient (10-12) | Developing (5-9) | Insufficient (0-4) |
|----------|----------------|-------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Working Code** | Live demo of core functionality. Multiple features shown. Minimal bugs. Demonstrates MVP is achievable. | Demo works but limited features OR noticeable bugs. Core concept proven. | Demo very minimal (hello world level) OR significant bugs prevent demonstration of value. | No demo OR demo completely non-functional OR pre-recorded video instead of live demo. |

**What we're looking for:**
- **LIVE demo** (not screenshots, not video of it working last night)
- Shows at least 2 features working (even if basic)
- Demonstrates the core concept of your platform
- Proves you can write code together as a team

**Examples of acceptable minimal demos:**
- Task Manager: User can create a workspace, add a task, mark it complete
- E-Commerce: User can view products, add to cart, see cart total
- Recommendation: User can rate items, see "popular items" list
- Health Tracker: User can log a measurement, see it in a simple chart

**Examples of insufficient demos:**
- Just a login page with no functionality
- A static mockup with no backend
- "It works on my machine" (but not in the live demo)

### Demo Presentation (6 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (6) | Proficient (4) | Developing (2) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Demo Narration** | Clear narration of what's being shown. Explains what's happening as you click. Confident navigation. | Some narration. Mostly clear what's happening. | Minimal narration. Audience confused about what they're seeing. | No explanation of demo OR completely unclear what's being demonstrated. |

**What we're looking for:**
- Talk while you demo ("I'm clicking 'Add Task' which opens this form...")
- Explain what the audience is seeing
- If something breaks, recover gracefully
- Don't apologize for minimalism - it's Week 4!

### Technical Recovery (4 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (4) | Proficient (2-3) | Insufficient (0-1) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Handling Technical Issues** | If demo fails, team has backup (screenshots, explains what SHOULD happen) and recovers smoothly. | If demo fails, some recovery attempt but awkward. | Demo fails completely with no backup plan or explanation. |

**What we're looking for:**
- Do you have a backup plan if wifi dies?
- Can you explain what should happen if a bug appears?
- Do you stay calm and professional?

## 🏗️ SECTION 4: TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE (15 points)

You must explain HOW your system works, not just WHAT it does.

### Architecture Diagram (8 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (8) | Proficient (5-6) | Developing (3-4) | Insufficient (0-2) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Technical Architecture** | Clear diagram showing frontend, backend, database. Technologies identified. Data flow explained. | Diagram present but missing components or unclear connections. | Very vague diagram OR text description only. | No architecture explanation OR completely nonsensical. |

**Required elements:**
- A visual diagram (not just verbal description)
- Shows frontend technology (HTML/CSS/JS, React, etc.)
- Shows backend technology (Python/Flask, Node.js, etc.)
- Shows database (PostgreSQL, MySQL, etc.)
- Shows how data flows between components

**Example of what we want to see:**
```
[Browser] ←→ [Flask API] ←→ [PostgreSQL]
    ↓              ↓              ↓
 HTML/CSS      Python         Schema
```

### Database Schema (4 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (4) | Proficient (2-3) | Developing (1) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Database Design** | Key tables identified with relationships shown. Demonstrates understanding of data model. | Tables mentioned but relationships unclear. | Vague mention of database. | No database discussion OR clearly haven't designed schema yet. |

**What we're looking for:**
- What are your main tables/entities?
- How do they relate (one-to-many, many-to-many)?
- Does this schema make sense for the platform?

### Technology Justification (3 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (3) | Proficient (2) | Developing (1) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Stack Choices** | Clear explanation of why you chose your tech stack. Reasonable justifications. | Technologies mentioned but justification weak or missing. | No justification provided. | Chose technologies that clearly won't work for the project. |

**What we're looking for:**
- Why Flask vs. Node.js (or whatever you chose)?
- Why PostgreSQL vs. MySQL?
- Have you thought about this, or just picked randomly?

## 🤝 SECTION 5: TEAM DYNAMICS (10 points)

We're evaluating how you work together as a team.

### Balanced Participation (6 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (6) | Proficient (4) | Developing (2) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|----------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Everyone Speaks** | All team members present part of the pitch. Roughly equal speaking time. Smooth handoffs. | Most members speak but unbalanced (one person dominates). | 1-2 members do ALL the talking. | One person gives entire presentation while others stand silently. |

**What we're looking for:**
- Did everyone talk for at least 1 minute?
- Are handoffs smooth ("Now [Name] will explain our database design...")?
- Does it feel like a team presentation or one person's show?

### Professionalism & Cohesion (4 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (4) | Proficient (2-3) | Developing (1) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Team Presence** | Team dressed appropriately. Everyone attentive when not speaking. Supportive body language. | Mostly professional. Some inattention or awkwardness. | Casual to point of unprofessional OR clear disengagement from non-speaking members. | Actively unprofessional (e.g., arguing with each other, phone use). |

**What we're looking for:**
- Are you dressed like you're pitching to stakeholders (not like you just woke up)?
- Are you paying attention when teammates speak?
- Do you look like a team, or random people forced together?

## 💬 SECTION 6: Q&A HANDLING (5 points)

After your 8-minute presentation, you'll answer questions for 2 minutes.

### Question Response (5 points)

| Criteria | Excellent (5) | Proficient (3-4) | Developing (1-2) | Insufficient (0) |
|----------|--------------|------------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Q&A Performance** | Clear, direct answers. Team collaborates on responses. Admits "we don't know" if necessary. Professional tone. | Answers provided but may be unclear or defensive. | Deflects questions or provides clearly incorrect information. | Refuses to answer, argues with questioner, or completely unprepared for obvious questions. |

**Common questions you should be ready for:**
- "Why did you choose this platform over the others?"
- "How will you handle [specific technical challenge]?"
- "Who is your target user?"
- "What's your backup plan if [feature] is too hard?"
- "How are you dividing the work?"

**Good answer:** "We chose task management because three of us have freelance experience and personally felt this pain point. We're planning to handle multi-tenancy by filtering all queries by workspace_id..."

**Bad answer:** "Uh... we just picked it..." or "We'll figure that out later..."

## 💡 TIPS FOR SUCCESS

### Do This:
✅ Practice out loud with a timer  
✅ Start with the problem (engage audience emotionally)  
✅ Make personas specific and memorable  
✅ Demo even if it's minimal (working beats perfect)  
✅ Have a backup plan for technical failures  
✅ Divide speaking time equally  
✅ Make eye contact with the audience  
✅ Speak loudly and clearly  
✅ Show enthusiasm for your project  

### Don't Do This:
❌ Wing it without practice  
❌ Start with "Hi we're Team X" before stating the problem  
❌ Use generic personas ("college students who need organization")  
❌ Apologize for minimal MVP ("We know this isn't much...")  
❌ Let one person dominate the presentation  
❌ Read slides word-for-word  
❌ Argue with questioners during Q&A  
❌ Go over time (respect the audience)

## 🤔 FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

**Q: What if our demo doesn't work on presentation day?**  
A: Have backup screenshots and explain what SHOULD happen. You'll lose some points but not all. This is why we grade "Technical Recovery."

**Q: Can we use slides from a template?**  
A: Yes! Focus on content, not graphic design (unless you have time). Clearness > beauty.

**Q: How technical should the architecture explanation be?**  
A: Explain it like you're talking to a technical manager who understands databases and APIs but doesn't know your specific code. Avoid jargon, but don't dumb it down.

**Q: What if we changed our platform after Week 1?**  
A: Talk to instructor ASAP. Late changes are risky but sometimes necessary.

**Q: Do we present to just the instructor or the whole class?**  
A: Whole class (and potentially design partners). Assume a technical audience.

**Q: What if one team member is absent on presentation day?**  
A: Remaining members must cover their parts. Notify instructor immediately. Absent member receives 0 unless excused.

**Q: Can we bring notes?**  
A: Yes, but don't read from them. Use as a safety net, not a script.

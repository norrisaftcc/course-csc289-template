# THE REALITY CHECK

## What This Course Actually Is (Read Before Week 1)

**Time to read:** 8 minutes
**Tone:** Direct, out of character, no satire
**Purpose:** Set expectations that lead to growth

-----

## The Uncomfortable Truth About “C’s Get Degrees”

Yes, a C will get you a diploma. It will not get you a job.

Here’s what happens in a technical interview:

> “Tell me about a project you worked on.”
>
> “Walk me through how you solved a problem.”
>
> “Show me your GitHub. What’s your most interesting commit?”

The interviewer is not checking your GPA. They’re checking whether you can **think**, **build**, and **communicate**. These skills are not faked. They’re earned through practice—specifically, through repeated attempts, failures, and iterations.

A student who commits every day, even small things, even broken things, and iterates until they work demonstrates something valuable: **they know how to grow**.

A student who submits polished work once and disappears demonstrates something else: they might have gotten lucky, or gotten help, or gotten AI to do it. There’s no evidence they can do it again.

**Your GitHub history is your actual transcript.** Employers increasingly look at it. They can see:

- How often you work
- How you respond to feedback
- Whether you iterate or give up
- How you communicate in commits and PRs

This course is designed to build that history.

## What We Actually Measure

We don’t measure perfection. We measure **growth velocity**.

**Growth velocity** = how fast you’re improving, not how good you are right now.

This means:

|Student A |Student B |
|--------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
|Starts strong, stays the same |Starts weak, improves steadily |
|Perfect first submission, no iteration|Messy first attempt, three revisions, solid final|
|Uses AI, submits output as-is |Uses AI, then iterates and understands |
|**Lower growth velocity** |**Higher growth velocity** |

Student B wins. Not because we’re “being nice to beginners,” but because Student B is demonstrating the skill that actually matters: **the ability to learn and improve**.

Employers can teach you their tech stack. They cannot teach you how to grow.

## The Game and the Skills

This course uses an “AlgoCratic Futures” frame—a satirical corporate dystopia with clearance levels, absurd memos, and “The Algorithm.”

**The satire is the vehicle. The skills are real.**

Here’s the breakdown:

- **95% of your time** = learning genuine technical and professional skills
- **5% of your time** = navigating the narrative frame

The clearance system (INFRARED → RED → ORANGE → etc.) tracks your progression through increasingly complex challenges. It’s gamification, yes, but it’s measuring real competence:

|Clearance|What It Means |
|---------|----------------------------------------------------------|
|INFRARED |You have a working environment and can follow instructions|
|RED |You can build simple things independently |
|ORANGE |You can handle ambiguity and make decisions |
|YELLOW+ |You can lead, mentor, and handle complex systems |

These map directly to what employers call “junior,” “mid-level,” and “senior” developer capabilities.

## Attendance: What It Actually Is

Let’s be direct: the attendance policy isn’t about butts in seats. It’s about **professional accountability**.

Here’s how it works in industry:

- **Present and shipping?** Great. You’re doing your job.
- **Remote but shipping and communicating?** Also great. Remote work is real.
- **Present but not shipping?** Problem. You’re consuming space without producing value.
- **Absent and silent?** Career-ending behavior. This is how people get fired.

The course mirrors this:

### The Policy

**8 unexcused absences = removal from course.**

This sounds harsh. It’s actually more lenient than most workplaces, where 8 unexplained absences would get you fired far earlier.

### What Counts as Excused

An excused absence requires:

1. **Communication** — An email explaining why you’re not present
1. **Evidence of continued work** — A link to your most recent GitHub activity (PR, commit, issue)

You can’t use the same PR twice in a row. Each absence needs to show you’re still working, just not physically present.

This is exactly how remote work functions professionally. You’re learning the skill while demonstrating the skill.

### Why This Matters

“I was there but didn’t do anything” is worse than “I wasn’t there but shipped code.”

We’re measuring engagement with the work, not just physical presence. But if you’re neither present nor working, there’s no engagement to measure.

## AI Use: The Real Policy

**You are expected to use AI tools.** This is 2026. Pretending AI doesn’t exist is malpractice.

However:

**AI output ≠ your work.** AI output + iteration + understanding = your work.

The metrics system will detect:

- Single large commits with no iteration (suspicious)
- Code style that doesn’t match your previous work (suspicious)
- Perfect submissions with no history of struggle (very suspicious)

The metrics system will reward:

- Multiple commits showing evolution
- Evidence of debugging and fixing
- Questions asked in PRs or discussions
- Helping others with what you’ve learned

**The winning strategy:** Use AI to get unstuck, then make sure you understand what it gave you. Iterate on it. Break it and fix it. Commit the journey, not just the destination.

## What Success Looks Like

By the end of this course, a successful student will have:

1. **A GitHub history** showing consistent work over 16 weeks
1. **Evidence of growth** — early work worse than late work, demonstrably
1. **Collaboration receipts** — PR reviews, help given, help received
1. **Portfolio artifacts** — things they can show in interviews
1. **The ability to articulate what they learned** — not just “I built X” but “I started not knowing Y, struggled with Z, and now understand W”

Notice what’s not on this list: a perfect GPA, zero bugs, or never needing help.

## The Social Contract

Here’s what we commit to:

**The course commits to:**

- Clear expectations (this document)
- Measurement that rewards growth over perfection
- Support when you’re struggling (if you communicate)
- A learning environment where failure is safe
- Skills that transfer to actual employment

**You commit to:**

- Showing up (physically or through demonstrated work)
- Communicating when you’re stuck or overwhelmed
- Iterating when things don’t work
- Helping peers when you can
- Taking the growth opportunity seriously

This is a two-way relationship. We can’t help you if you disappear.

## The Bottom Line

This course is designed to change you—to install habits and skills that will serve you for decades. That change requires effort, discomfort, and iteration.

You can coast through and get a C. You’ll learn almost nothing, build no portfolio, and interview poorly. The diploma will be technically valid and practically useless.

Or you can engage fully, struggle, iterate, and grow. You’ll finish with skills, artifacts, and habits that employers recognize instantly.

**The choice is yours.** But you should make it consciously, knowing what each path costs.

## Questions You Might Have

**“What if I’m already good at this?”**
Then your growth velocity will be measured in complexity, not basics. You’ll be expected to take on harder challenges, mentor peers, and produce more sophisticated work. Being ahead doesn’t mean coasting.

**“What if I’m really behind?”**
Then you have the highest growth potential. A student who goes from “barely functional” to “competent” demonstrates more learning than one who goes from “competent” to “slightly more competent.” The metrics reward your trajectory.

**“What if I have a crisis mid-semester?”**
Communicate immediately. We have accommodations, extensions, reduced scope options, and counseling referrals. What we can’t do is help you if we don’t know you need help. Silence compounds problems.

**“What if my team has problems?”**
Escalate within one week. This is not “snitching”—it’s how help arrives. The instructor has resources you don’t. Use them.

**“Is the satire required?”**
Engaging with the frame makes the experience more memorable and fun. But we’re measuring your skills, not your acting ability. Focus on the work; the satire is seasoning.
